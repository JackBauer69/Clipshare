# DRAFT — Commercial Source-Code and Derivative-Product Licensing Policy

**Planning document only. This is not an offer or grant of permission.**

## Intended policy

The copyright holder of ClipShare's original code wants official ClipShare application use to remain free (including internal business use), but wants **advance written authorization** before a third party incorporates protected ClipShare code into a product that they distribute or sell.

A commercial license may be negotiated individually. Potential terms to agree with the applicant:

1. Which exact ClipShare source files, code versions, trademarks, and assets may be used.
2. Whether modifications, derivative products, redistribution, sublicensing, and commercial sales are authorized.
3. License fee or royalty (if any), territory, duration, delivery model, attribution, and reporting.
4. Responsibility for support, updates, security, third-party notices, and warranty/liability.
5. Whether the licensee must disclose a source-code offer, branding, or modifications.
6. Termination, breach remediation, and transfer/change-of-control rules.

**Not restricted by ClipShare copyright alone:** independently authored products that share an idea or functionality but do not copy protected ClipShare expression.

## Release prerequisites

- Verify legal ownership/rights for all original project contributions.
- Commission legal review of the final end-user agreement and any commercial license template.
- Verify third-party and bundled components' license compliance.
- Decide how official installers display license terms and record acceptance, where applicable.
- Approve exact owner/legal-entity identity, contact address, and distribution terms.
- Align RPM license metadata and GitHub release gates with the adopted legal terms.

Until approved documents are published, the repository's [LICENSING.md](../../LICENSING.md) remains the operative description of current *status*, not a grant of new permissions.
